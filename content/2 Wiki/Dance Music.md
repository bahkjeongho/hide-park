- [Alternative Dance](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/alternative-dance/)
    
    Incorporates rhythms and synthesizers from Electronic Dance Music within the context of Alternative Rock and related styles; predominantly popular in the 1990s.

- [Disco](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/disco/)
    
    Combines the lush orchestration of Philly Soul and bass grooves of Funk with a four-on-the-floor rhythm.
    
    - [Boogie](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/boogie/)
        
        Continuation of the Disco sound with increased influence from Funk, Synth Funk, and R&B / Soul / Funk and a slower tempo, often featuring drum machines and filter modulated synthesizers.
        
    
    - [Electro-Disco](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/electro-disco/)
        
        Disco instrumentation is largely - if not entirely - replaced by synthesizers and augmented with futuristic elements such as the vocoder.
        
        - [Hi-NRG](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hi-nrg/)
            
            Emerged in the late 1970s and quickly became the most popular form of Electronic Dance Music in the LGBT club scenes of San Francisco and New York, characterized by its uptempo, high-energy sound.
    
    - [Euro-Disco](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/euro-disco/)
        
        European style that moved away from the genre's Rhythm & Blues and Soul roots, speeding up the tempo and adding Electronic instruments like synthesizers and drum machines as a backing for more melodic Europop-influenced vocals.
    
    
    - [Nu-Disco](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/nu-disco/)
        
        21st-century trend mixing modern, polished Electronic production with the aesthetics, structures, and lively, organic beats of 1970s and 1980s Disco.
        

- [Electronic Dance Music](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/electronic-dance-music/)
    
    Broad category for subgenres mainly derived from Disco, featuring Electronic sounds, synthesizers, drum machines and varying BPM ranges.
    
    - [Breakbeat](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/breakbeat/)
        
        Syncopated drum patterns (breakbeats), generally driving the groove at 110-160 BPM, emerging in the late 1980s from sampling drum breaks, before forming an umbrella genre separate from Drum and Bass, Downtempo, and Hip Hop.
        
        - [Acid Breaks](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/acid-breaks/)
            
            Incorporates prominent "squelching", resonant low pass filter sounds typical to Acid House, and often other "trippy" effects into intense, syncopated, broken rhythms in the tempos of 110-150 BPM.
            
        
        - [Baltimore Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/baltimore-club/)
            
            Drew on the "booty bass" syncopation of Miami Bass, combined with Breakbeat rhythms around 130 BPM and chopped up vocal samples as found in Ghetto House.
            
        
        - [Big Beat](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/big-beat/)
            
            Popular in Britain during the late 1990s, driven by edited breakbeats and prominent basslines.
            
        
        - [Breakbeat Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/breakbeat-hardcore/)
            
            Originated from the combination of sampled breakbeats and energetic rhythms with the "rave" influences set by early-1990s Techno and Acid House, including upbeat "stab" riffing and sample-based production.
    
    - [Bubblegum Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/bubblegum-bass/)
        
        Originated in the early 2010s, exaggerating the cutest and most feminine parts of 2000s Pop and combining them with bouncy, plastic synths over rhythms and sounds derived from a variety of contemporary club styles.
    
    - [Dariacore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/dariacore/)
        
        SoundCloud-based scene developed in the early 2020s, combining prominent vocal samples and distorted, dense production with influences from a wide range of Electronic Dance Music genres, often incorporating musical and visual references to popular media and internet culture.
    
    - [Deconstructed Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/deconstructed-club/)
        
        Emerged in the 2010s as the hybridization of diverse Electronic Dance Music genres with an abrasive Post-Industrial sound.
        
    
    - [Drum and Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/drum-and-bass/)
        
        Fast, syncopated breakbeat patterns (often sampled or programmed) with prominent basslines, commonly within the 160-180 BPM range.
        
        - [Atmospheric Drum and Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/atmospheric-drum-and-bass/)
            
            Prominent in the mid-1990s, uses faster, de-emphasized breakbeats, and places a greater focus on melodic elements such as slow, warm basslines and atmospheric melodies inspired by Ambient and Ambient House.
            
        
        - [Footwork Jungle](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/footwork-jungle/)
            
            Combining Footwork's archetypal percussion, skittering drum fills, handclaps, and syncopated rhythms, with Drum and Bass drum programming and breakbeats, merging the two into unconventional, frantic drumming and dense, off-kilter rhythms.
            
        - [Jungle](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/jungle/)
            
            Developed in the early 1990s from Breakbeat Hardcore, featuring rapid, chaotic-sounding breakbeats and strong basslines; known as the forerunner of most Drum and Bass styles.
        
        - [Liquid Drum and Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/liquid-drum-and-bass/)
            
            Atmospheric and melodic genre that developed in the early 2000s; utilizes polished and smooth production, simpler structures, and originally sampled Funk and Soul.
            
        
        - [Minimal Drum and Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/minimal-drum-and-bass/)
            
            Minimalist, sparse sound; emerged in two main 2000s strains, one Dubstep-inspired and one IDM-inspired, which crossed paths in the 2010s.
    
    - [Dubstep](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/dubstep/)
        
        Half-time syncopated and sparse beats set around 140 BPM with heavy sub-basses; emerged in South London in the early 2000s.
        
        - [Brostep](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/brostep/)
            
            Offshoot of Dubstep which replaces its sub-bass-oriented sound with an emphasis on catchy or aggressive mid-range melodies.
            
        - [Riddim](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/riddim/)
            
            Repetitive and relatively simplistic style inspired by mid-to-late-2000s UK Dubstep, often using robotic and/or squishy sound design along with bouncy square basses and less prominent buildups.
            
    
    - [East Coast Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/east-coast-club/)
        
        Group of club styles from the US East Coast that combines Miami Bass rhythms with influence from Breakbeat and Ghetto House.
        
        - [Baltimore Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/baltimore-club/)
            
            Drew on the "booty bass" syncopation of Miami Bass, combined with Breakbeat rhythms around 130 BPM and chopped up vocal samples as found in Ghetto House.
            
        
        - [Jersey Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/jersey-club/)
            
            Emerged in Newark in the late 2000s; features fast tempos, hard kicks, and less prominent breakbeats.
            
        
        - [Philly Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/philly-club/)
            
            Builds on Baltimore Club with a faster tempo and sometimes influences from more modern genres like Trap [EDM].
            
    
    - [EBM](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ebm/)
        
        Melodic and dancefloor-friendly take on Post-Industrial music that developed in the early 1980s.
        
        - [Dark Electro](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/dark-electro/)
            
            Emerged in the early 1990s in Europe, incorporating the abrasiveness of old school Industrial but with a greater focus on a horror-inspired atmosphere and harsh, distorted vocals.
        
        - [Futurepop](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/futurepop/)
            
            Derivative of EBM, retaining its apocalyptic worldview but incorporating heavy influence from the melodic style of Synthpop.
            
        
        - [New Beat](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/new-beat/)
            
            Emerged in the mid-1980s in Belgium; characterized by dark basslines, hard-hitting grooves and a slow tempo.
            
        - [TBM](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/tbm/)
            
            Emerged in the late 1990s and early 2000s; grew out of a combination of Techno and EBM.
            
    
    - [Electro](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/electro/)
        
        Syncopated rhythms programmed on the Roland TR-808 drum machine, often resembling Funk breaks, along with simple funky basslines.
        
    
    - [Electroclash](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/electroclash/)
        
        Draws from 1980s styles such as Electro, New Wave, and Electro-Disco combined with Techno, resulting in minimal, throbbing beats and basslines, buzzing synthesizer parts, vocoders and often monotone, half-spoken, half-sung vocals.
        
    
    - [Eurobeat](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/eurobeat/)
        
        Fast-paced with roots in Hi-NRG and Italo-Disco; became mainly popular in Japan in the 1990s.
    
    - [Eurodance](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/eurodance/)
        
        Takes elements of Euro-Disco, House, Techno, and Hi-NRG while featuring on-beat kick, hi-hat and snare sounds at around 110 to 150 bpm.
    
    - [Footwork](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/footwork/)
        
        Extensive use of drum fills, tom drums, handclaps, and snares within fast-paced, highly syncopated beats not bound to a 4/4 kick rhythm.
        
        - [Footwork Jungle](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/footwork-jungle/)
            
            Combining Footwork's archetypal percussion, skittering drum fills, handclaps, and syncopated rhythms, with Drum and Bass drum programming and breakbeats, merging the two into unconventional, frantic drumming and dense, off-kilter rhythms.
            
    
    - [Funk mandelão](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/funk-mandelao/)
        
        Simple lyricism, raw and minimalistic production, and distinct drops with bursting bass.
        
        - [Beat bruxaria](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/beat-bruxaria/)
            
            São Paulo-originated Funk brasileiro with aggressive, distorted and high-pitched beats featuring minimalistic percussion and diversified sampling.
            
        
        - [Brazilian Phonk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/brazilian-phonk/)
            
            Merges Funk automotivo's rhythms, vocals, and energy, with the energetic and nocturnal production typical of the Drift Phonk scene, and Phonk House.
            
    
    - [Future Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/future-bass/)
        
        Woozy, detuned synthesizers, 7th chords, harsh basslines, syncopated percussion with fast-paced hi-hats and a melodic, carefree atmosphere.
        
        - [Kawaii Future Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/kawaii-future-bass/)
            
            Highly melodic style rooted in Anime and video game culture, associated with cute aesthetics and Chiptune-inspired synths.
        
    - [Ghettotech](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ghettotech/)
        
        Fuses the syncopation and synthesized sounds of Electro and Detroit Techno with the cut-up samples of Ghetto House and fast rhythms of Miami Bass.
        
    
    - [Glitch Hop [EDM]](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/glitch-hop-edm/)
        
        Tempos around 100–110 BPM with swung, bouncy drum patterns and a focus on upbeat, danceable drops and buildups.
        
        - [Ghetto Funk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ghetto-funk/)
            
            Rich sampling, drawing from classic Hip Hop as well as from Funk and Soul, and strong midtempo break grooves.
            
    
    - [Grime](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/grime/)
        
        UK Garage-derived production style characterized by dark, fast-paced, often aggressive beats that commonly features fast-paced MCing.
        
            
    
    - [Hardcore [EDM]](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardcore-edm/)
        
        Umbrella term for a wide variety of fast-paced and energetic Electronic Dance Music styles that have been developed since the early 1990s.
        
        - [Acidcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/acidcore/)
            
            Developed out of the 1990s Acid Techno scene utilizing its prominence of "acid" TB-303 bassline sequences into more intense rhythms derived from earlier Hardcore [EDM], Gabber and Trance music.
            
    
        - [Belgian Techno](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/belgian-techno/)
            
            Early form of Hardcore [EDM], resulting from the further evolution of New Beat due to the strong influence of Techno and EBM.
            
        
        - [Breakbeat Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/breakbeat-hardcore/)
            
            Originated from the combination of sampled breakbeats and energetic rhythms with the "rave" influences set by early-1990s Techno and Acid House, including upbeat "stab" riffing and sample-based production.
            
        
        - [Breakcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/breakcore/)
            
            Mixes Hardcore [EDM] with cut-up, complex, and frequently noisy breakbeats at very high tempos inspired by Jungle and Drum and Bass, often with heavy use of sampling.
            
        
        - [Gabber](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/gabber/)
            
            Fast-paced and energetic, often in the 160-220 BPM range, with a hallmark of a distinctive distorted kick sound.
            
        
        - [Happy Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/happy-hardcore/)
            
            Emerged in the 1990s from the UK Breakbeat Hardcore scene; characterized by saccharine vocals, uplifting piano riffs and spacey effects, gradually developing to emphasize extremely fast-paced four-on-the-floor beats.
            
            
            - [UK Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/uk-hardcore/)
                
                Four-on-the-floor beat around 175 BPM, thick, prominent basslines, and uplifting synth leads, with a cleaner and more accessible sound than other forms of Hardcore [EDM].
                
            
        - [Hardtek](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardtek/)
            
            Modern version of the "tribecore" sound featuring deep alternating basses and festival EDM-influenced drops.
            
            - [Raggatek](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/raggatek/)
                
                Influenced by Reggae, Ragga and Dancehall, combining half-time instrumental sections with Hardtek's fast four-on-the-floor rhythms, as well as vocal samples, clean basslines and skank rhythms.
                
        
        - [Industrial Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/industrial-hardcore/)
            
            Forms which incorporate mechanical sound design, high levels of distortion, and dark atmospheres, often at midtempo or fast tempos and commonly based from elements of Gabber, Darkcore and Speedcore.
            
        
        - [Speedcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/speedcore/)
            
            Exceedingly fast tempos, frequently above 300 BPM, and often aggressive themes and samples.
        
    
    - [Hard Dance](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hard-dance/)
        
        Umbrella term for EDM mainly developed out of Hard Trance and UK Hard House; usually characterized by fast tempos, hard kick drums and a "bouncy" syncopated groove.
        
        - [Hardstyle](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardstyle/)
            
            Hard, four-on-the-floor kick drums, fast tempo around 150 BPM, reverse bass and harsh, distorted synths.
            
        
        - [Hardtek](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardtek/)
            
            Modern version of the "tribecore" sound featuring deep alternating basses and festival EDM-influenced drops.
            
            - [Raggatek](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/raggatek/)
                
                Influenced by Reggae, Ragga and Dancehall, combining half-time instrumental sections with Hardtek's fast four-on-the-floor rhythms, as well as vocal samples, clean basslines and skank rhythms.
                
        
        - [Hard Trance](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hard-trance/)
            
            Rave-oriented form of Trance infusing its atmospheres with faster, thumping rhythms and strong melodic / bassline sections, initially spawning out of the first wave in early-1990s Western Europe.
            
        
        - [Jumpstyle](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/jumpstyle/)
            
            Features a heavy 909 kick drum, four-on-the-floor beat, and a tempo between 140 and 150 bpm.
            
            - [Frenchtek](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/frenchtek/)
                
                Dark, dissonant, and minimal, inspired by Techno rhythms; emerged in Northern France in the early 2000s.
                
        
        - [UK Hardcore](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/uk-hardcore/)
            
            Four-on-the-floor beat around 175 BPM, thick, prominent basslines, and uplifting synth leads, with a cleaner and more accessible sound than other forms of Hardcore [EDM].
            
            - [Future Core](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/future-core/)
                
                Blends the speed, basslines, and rhythms of UK Hardcore (mostly coming from the J-core sphere) with euphoric, woozy synths, 7th chords and arpeggios, and vocal chops of Future Bass, particularly Kawaii Future Bass.
                
            
            - [Powerstomp](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/powerstomp/)
                
                Prominent, "stomping", and slightly distorted kick, relentless, repetitive reverse basses on the offbeats (borrowed from Hardstyle) that drive the tracks, giving the genre its distinct energetic sound, and cacophonous, noisy riffs and synth leads.
                
        
        - [UK Hard House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/uk-hard-house/)
            
            Defined by offbeat bass patterns, compressed 909-style drums, buildup-drop structures, hoover sounds, and tempos between 120 and 155 BPM.
            
            - [Scouse House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/scouse-house/)
                
                Features an upbeat and energetic sound often described as being "bouncy", influenced somewhat by Happy Hardcore.
                
                - [Hardbass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardbass/)
                    
                    Bouncy hard bass beats, fast tempo, usually 150-175 BPM, and occasional lyrics or rapping.
                    
            
    
    - [Hard Drum](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hard-drum/)
        
        Highly percussive, syncopated rhythms, influences from global contemporary genres, and lack of melodic elements, with a tempo lingering around 130 BPM.
        
    
    - [Hardtekk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardtekk/)
        
        German style stemming from Hard Techno and Hardcore [EDM], featuring highly distorted, bass-heavy kicks with snares at each backbeat, often set around 150-200 BPM.
        
    
    - [House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/house/)
        
        Generally features a four-on-the-floor beat within 110-140 BPM, a groove built on swing and syncopation, synthesized basslines, repetitive melodic elements, and an upbeat mood.
        
        - [Acid House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/acid-house/)
            
            Emerged in the mid-1980s, characterised by repetitive basslines created with the distinctive modulated "squelching" sound of the Roland TB-303 synthesiser.
            
        
        - [Afro House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/afro-house/)
            
            Emerged by fusing Deep House with Southern African Music, characterized by hypnotizing drum patterns of shakers and congas, often sampled African vocals or soulful original features, and spiritual, organic, melodic atmosphere.
            
            - [3-Step](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/3-step/)
                
                Distinct three-kick-drum rhythm, blends influences from Amapiano and Broken Beat, resulting in relaxed, freeform, playful, Jazz-like feel.
                
        
        - [Amapiano](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/amapiano/)
            
            House from South Africa deriving from previous popular genres including Gqom, Kwaito, and Deep House.
            
        
        - [Ambient House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ambient-house/)
            
            Primarily British genre developed in the 1990s with beats, synthesizers, and vocals in styles similar to House, treated with effects that are often described as "dream-like" or "chilled out".
            
        
        - [Ballroom](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ballroom/)
            
            Hard-hitting rhythms, with generally improvised chanting by commentator MCs and distinctive crash samples; emerged from predominantly Black and LGBT culture in Harlem.
            
        
        - [Baltimore Club](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/baltimore-club/)
            
            Drew on the "booty bass" syncopation of Miami Bass, combined with Breakbeat rhythms around 130 BPM and chopped up vocal samples as found in Ghetto House.
            
        
        - [Bass House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/bass-house/)
            
            Busy, punchy percussion, prominent basslines and dirty, distorted synths resembling other bass music genres, with influences including Brostep, Fidget House, Bassline, Speed Garage, and Tech House.
            
            - [Speed House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/speed-house/)
                
                Mixes busy, dirty sound design and intense drums derived from Bass House with fast tempos from 140-160 BPM, pumping basslines, and other UK Hard House influences.
                
        
        - [Bassline](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/bassline/)
            
            Strong emphasis on intricate basslines as well as four-on-the-floor beat patterns.
            
        
        - [Big Room House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/big-room-house/)
            
            Energetic, festival-oriented style characterized by heavy reverb, intense build-ups, strong kicks, minimalistic drop melodies, and sparse, raw breakdowns.
            
        
        - [Brazilian Bass](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/brazilian-bass/)
            
            Originated in the 2010s in Brazil, taking influences from Deep House and Tech House, characterized by deep, punchy basslines making use of detuning and filtering effects.
            
        
        - [Chicago Hard House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/chicago-hard-house/)
            
            Originated from Chicago around the mid-1990s as a development of Ghetto House, emphasizing hard-hitting, off-beat drum patterns, fast tempos, and lack of melodies.
            
        
        - [Chicago House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/chicago-house/)
            
            Umbrella term for a broad variety of House that originated from Chicago in the (mostly mid-to-late) 1980s and early 90s.
            
        
        - [Deep House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/deep-house/)
            
            Emerged in the United States during the mid-1980s, featuring a relaxed tempo and influences from Jazz, Funk, and Soul.
            
            
        
        - [Electro House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/electro-house/)
            
            Warm analog basslines, punchy percussion, crisp high-pitched synthesizer leads, and clipped vocal samples.
            
            - [Complextro](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/complextro/)
                
                Features densely-layered sound design, fast-paced cut-up sections, aggressive stabs, and common crossover with Brostep and Fidget House.
                
            
            - [Dutch House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/dutch-house/)
                
                High-pitched wonky synth melodies and heavy influence from Fidget House which developed in the Netherlands around 2007-08.
                
            
            - [French Electro](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/french-electro/)
                
                Mixes Electro House with major elements of genres such as Electroclash, Nu-Disco, Synth Funk, and even Electro-Industrial, resulting in a harsh, punchy, and funky midtempo sound.
                
        
        - [Eletrofunk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/eletrofunk/)
            
            Blends Funk brasileiro vocals with production from popular late 2000s and early 2010s House.
            
        
        - [Euro House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/euro-house/)
            
            Vocal-focused style emerging in the late 1980s in Europe, developing in parallel with Eurodance.
            
            - [Hardbag](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/hardbag/)
                
                Development of handbag / Diva House characterized by heavier, hard hitting percussion, more pronounced rave and early Hard Dance elements, and fewer vocals.
                
        
        - [French House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/french-house/)
            
            Originated in France in the mid-1990s, characterized by the use of filtered (low-pass, phaser, etc.) samples of Funk and Disco.
            
        
        - [Funky House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/funky-house/)
            
            Funky, warm, catchy basslines on top of looped Funk or Disco samples, resulting in an upbeat, bouncy, and pacy groove.
            
        
        - [Future Funk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/future-funk/)
            
            Sample-based style influenced by French House and Synth Funk that utilizes Vaporwave editing techniques.
            
        
        - [Future House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/future-house/)
            
            Features distinctive metallic-sounding drops and frequency-modulated basslines at around 120-130 BPM.
        
        - [Garage House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/garage-house/)
            
            Disco-influenced style of House that developed in the 1980s, incorporating elements of Gospel, Soul, and Dub, before evolving into a more upbeat style that prominently features crisp syncopated hi-hats and synth stabs.
        
        - [Ghetto House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/ghetto-house/)
            
            Rough sound based around minimal drum machine-driven tracks and heavy usage of vocal samples.
            
        
        - [Italo House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/italo-house/)
            
            Originated in Italy during the late 1980s, making use of anthemic pianos, diva vocals, and Dance-Pop song structures.
            
        
        - [Latin House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/latin-house/)
            
            Incorporates elements of Latin music (various Hispanic American Music and Brazilian Music genres), including their melodies, vocals, and instruments, as well as additional Latin percussion.
            
        
        - [Melodic House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/melodic-house/)
            
            Arpeggiated synth lines, focus on a chilled and laid-back atmosphere, and influence from genres such as Progressive House and Deep House.
            
        
        - [Microhouse](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/microhouse/)
            
            Stripped-down, minimal form of House, originating in the late 90s, which takes influence from Minimal Techno, Glitch and IDM.
            
        
        - [Progressive House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/progressive-house/)
            
            Trance-influenced strain of House that grew to prominence in the early 1990s, using polished production and gradual progressions.
        
        - [Romanian Popcorn](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/romanian-popcorn/)
            
            Emerged in the second half of the 2000s in Romania, often featuring brass melodies and "staccato" pluck grooves.
        
        - [Tech House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/tech-house/)
            
            Emerged in the mid-1990s taking influences from Techno, broadly characterized by minimalistic and repetitive melodies, focus on the bassline, and constant groove recognizable by prominent off-beat hi-hats.
            
            - [Deep Tech](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/deep-tech/)
                
                Incorporates UK Garage rhythms and synths stabs, influences from British rave music, UK Bass and UK Deep House, resulting in an urban, subdued, but danceable sound with deep, muted basslines.
                
        
        - [Tribal House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/tribal-house/)
            
            Prominent, syncopated rhythms of tribal drums such as toms and bongos on top of a House beat, usually resulting in a percussive, energetic sound, while ranging in mood.
            
        
        - [Tropical House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/tropical-house/)
            
            Offshoot of Deep House featuring slow tempos around 100-115 BPM, light percussion and acoustic instrumentation, setting out to create a relaxed, summery, beach-like atmosphere.
            
        
        - [UK Hard House](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/uk-hard-house/)
            
            Defined by offbeat bass patterns, compressed 909-style drums, buildup-drop structures, hoover sounds, and tempos between 120 and 155 BPM.
            
    - [Moombahton](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/moombahton/)
        
        Offshoot of Dutch House characterized by tempos in the 100-110 BPM range, Reggaetón-influenced rhythms, bass-heavy kicks and high-pitched synths.
    
    - [Nu-Disco](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/nu-disco/)
        
        21st-century trend mixing modern, polished Electronic production with the aesthetics, structures, and lively, organic beats of 1970s and 1980s Disco.
    
    - [Slimepunk](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/slimepunk/)
        
        Loud synth leads with a distorted, gritty, and "slimy" sound design, usually laid on top of production inspired by or taken directly from genres such as Halftime, Trap [EDM], Brostep, and other forms of 2010s EDM.
        
    
    - [Techno](https://rateyourmusic.com/genre/techno/)
